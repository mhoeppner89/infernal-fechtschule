# Historical method and fictional boundary

## Objective

Historical grounding should create distinctive combat behavior and invite further reading. The game should avoid presenting reconstruction choices, modern terminology, or supernatural story elements as settled historical fact.

## Three labels

Every move, character claim, codex statement, image, and story element should carry one internal provenance label.

### Documented

Directly supported by a cited primary source or a reliable scholarly edition/translation. The shipped codex should identify the source location as precisely as licensing and format allow.

### Adapted

Based on documented principles or actions, but changed for arena movement, touch controls, horde readability, co-op, safety, or animation economy. The adaptation note states what changed.

### Invented

Created for game structure, character drama, balance, or supernatural fiction. It may use period visual language, but it is not presented as historical testimony.

The public interface can express these labels as concise icons or footnotes. Internal production records should retain full notes.

## Research record per master

Create one version-controlled dossier with:

```text
Master and dates
Primary source witnesses/editions
Reliable translations and scholarship
Weapon evidence
Core tactical principles
Candidate guards/actions
Disputed interpretations
Modern reconstruction references
Game adaptations
Invented material
Terminology decisions
Image and quotation licenses
Expert reviewers and review dates
```

The gameplay designer and historical reviewer should sign off separately. Agreement about the game’s function does not imply agreement about historical interpretation.

## Move-development workflow

1. Define the required gameplay function in plain terms: crowd starter, line closure, counter, armor opening, and so on.
2. Search the master’s sources for a relevant principle or action.
3. Record the source passage and translation provenance.
4. Decide whether the game action is documented, adapted, or invented.
5. Prototype with a neutral mechanical name.
6. Validate timing, silhouette, and route value before attaching a historical term.
7. Review the final animation and description together; labels can become misleading after animation changes.
8. Include a short codex note explaining the adaptation.

Do not force a historical label onto a move merely because production already animated it.

## Character portrayal

- Avoid reducing masters to national stereotypes or one anecdote.
- Build persona from teaching style, tactical emphasis, material context, and restrained fictional characterization.
- Distinguish documented biography from dramatic invention.
- Give antagonistic roles an explicit fictional frame.
- Do not imply direct encounters among people from different periods without acknowledging the journey premise.

## Marozzo and demons

The game’s demonic arc is fictional and inspired by early-modern printed ornament, grotesques, and the user’s proposed title-page interpretation. Public text should avoid claims that Achille Marozzo historically summoned demons.

Safer narrative framing:

- The infernal is anchored in places — a crypt beneath a castle, a cursed hall — not in printed books.
- Marozzo may be guardian, prisoner, interpreter, or avatar of what the chains hold.
- Grotesques from printed margins inspire the chained wretches loosed in the castle crypts.
- A codex entry shows the source image, its bibliographic information, and a separate note describing the game’s invention.

## Terminology policy

- Prefer established English descriptions in the live HUD.
- Use original-language terms where they add precision and have source support.
- Provide pronunciation and translation in the codex rather than crowding combat UI.
- Avoid inventing pseudo-historical German or Italian labels.
- Keep internal stable move IDs independent of changing display terminology.

## Source hierarchy

Use, in descending order of evidentiary role:

1. Primary treatise/manuscript/print witnesses
2. Critical editions and scholarly translations
3. Peer-reviewed scholarship and specialist monographs
4. Museum/library catalog records
5. Transparent modern reconstruction notes
6. Popular summaries only as discovery aids

Disagreement among credible interpretations should be recorded rather than silently resolved through one reconstruction community.

## Images and text

For every historical visual or quotation, record:

- Holding institution
- Shelfmark or identifier
- Edition/page/folio
- Creator and approximate date
- Source URL
- Rights statement and jurisdictional caveat
- File modifications
- Credit line

Public-domain status of the underlying historical work does not automatically settle rights in a modern scan, edition, translation, or photograph. Confirm each shipped asset.

## Expert review

At minimum, obtain:

- One reviewer familiar with the relevant source tradition
- One gameplay reviewer who was not involved in the move design
- Sensitivity/context review where national, religious, racialized, or demonic imagery could be misread

Record disagreements and the production decision. A reviewer credit must not imply endorsement beyond the material they reviewed.
